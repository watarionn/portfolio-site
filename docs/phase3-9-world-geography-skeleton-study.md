# Phase 3.9 - World Geography Skeleton Study

Updated: 2026-09-19
Status: geography family selected; exact geography remains unlocked

## Study result

Three abstract skeleton families were compared: Continental, Archipelago, and Ring. The generated comparison sheet is visual study evidence only. None of its coastlines, mountains, buildings, labels, scale bars, arrows, or exact district positions are production-approved.

## Candidate A - Continental

Strengths:
- strongest continuity between the four current districts,
- supports roads, rivers, valleys, coast, and highlands without forced transitions,
- multiple future expansion fronts are easy to preserve,
- works naturally with a large Master World and compact Initial Camera.

Risk:
- if too symmetrical, it can read as a generic RPG island built around four landmarks.

## Candidate B - Archipelago

Strengths:
- district identities can become visually distinct,
- new islands can emerge naturally from cloud as the portfolio grows,
- water travel and offshore expansion create strong future variety.

Risk:
- making every district an island weakens the sense that the current districts belong to one connected city.

## Candidate C - Ring

Strengths:
- distinctive world silhouette,
- strong central-water composition,
- easy visual orientation.

Risk:
- future growth is biased outward from the ring and becomes structurally repetitive.

## Selected geography family

Carry forward **Mainland + Archipelago Hybrid (H2)**.

H2 is not Candidate A with decorative islands. It is a connected primary land system whose coastline, peninsulas, channels, nearby islands, and offshore reserve all participate in navigation and future growth.

### H2 principles

- one dominant connected mainland supports the current Portfolio City,
- coastline is irregular and asymmetrical,
- at least one meaningful peninsula or coastal projection breaks the mainland silhouette,
- nearby islands exist but do not isolate every current district,
- internal water may appear as river, inlet, canal, lake, or bay without forcing a ring,
- highland terrain creates an observational region without requiring a central mountain,
- the four current districts should not form a literal cross or perfect circle,
- future growth can continue both over land and across water.

## Current district terrain roles

These are relationships, not coordinates.

### Observatory Hill
Place in an elevated region with visual access over part of the world. It may sit inland, on a ridge, or above a coast. Avoid making it the geometric center merely because it is elevated.

### Archive Street
Place on stable connected land where an older urban fabric can plausibly develop. It should have strong terrestrial connection to at least one other current district.

### Workshop Alley
Place on connected land with room for roads, infrastructure, yards, or terrain transitions. It should feel active rather than isolated.

### Waterside Play
Place directly against meaningful water: coast, inlet, river mouth, bay, canal district, or similar. Water must affect the district silhouette rather than act as distant decoration.

## Expansion-front model

Reserve three qualitatively different fronts.

### F-A Inland frontier
A road, valley, plain, forest corridor, or highland pass continuing from the mainland.

### F-B Coastal frontier
A peninsula, coast road, river mouth, bay edge, or shoreline transition.

### F-C Offshore frontier
An island group, bridge route, ferry-like visual connection, or other across-water destination.

District 5 and District 6 should preferably use different front types. Their exact directions and themes remain unlocked.

## Initial camera relationship

The Initial Camera shows the four current districts as one readable cluster plus portions of at least two frontier types. It does not show the whole H2 master geography.

Cloud should interrupt the world organically at ridges, coastlines, open sea, forests, or roads. Small terrain glimpses beyond cloud are desirable. The visitor should infer continuation rather than see an image boundary.

## Growth simulation

W0: four current districts on the connected published core; frontier glimpses hint at inland, coastal, and offshore continuation.

W1: District 5 opens one frontier and locally expands the Published World Bound. The camera may shift or widen only enough to preserve readable district scale.

W2: District 6 preferably opens a different frontier type, demonstrating that the world can branch rather than stretch into one corridor.

Later growth may turn a previously decorative island, peninsula, valley, or coast into meaningful published geography without moving existing district homes.
